# AI Models for Creative Writing — Landscape, Ratings & Research

**Compiled:** 26 July 2026
**Scope:** Frontier model comparison for fiction and non-fiction writing, plus the underlying research on why creative capability diverged from general capability.

## 1. Claude Fable 5 — 9.3

**Pros**
- Purpose-built for fiction rather than general capability. First frontier model positioned explicitly for prose.
- Leads creative benchmarks on voice, subtext, and character psychology.
- Sustains register across long passages rather than front-loading style in the opening paragraph.
- Best story critique and developmental feedback of the group.

**Cons**
- Expensive (~$10/$50 per M tokens on published boards).
- Safeguard routing sends some sessions to Opus 5 instead, which is disruptive mid-draft.
- Weaker tool use and reasoning than a general flagship.
- Too new for a substantial independent review base.

**Composite:** The strongest option for final-pass prose where voice is the deliverable. Not the model to run continuity passes with — you're paying a premium for a capability those tasks don't use.

## 2. Claude Opus 5 — 9.0

**Pros**
- Reported near-frontier quality at roughly half the cost per task versus the prior flagship.
- Prose close to Fable's with reasoning and tool use fully intact — matters when drafting sessions also involve research or structural work.
- Newest model in the lineup as of compilation date.

**Cons**
- Two days old at time of writing; effectively zero independent creative-writing evaluation exists.
- Not creative-tuned. Expect a more editorial default register than Fable.

**Composite:** The best single-model choice if you don't want to route. Watch for independent evaluation over the next quarter before treating the rating as settled.

## 3. GPT-5.6 (Sol) — 8.5

**Pros**
- Strongest structural thinker in the group: tighter outlines, fewer plot holes, better pacing distribution, cleaner cause-and-effect chains.
- Excellent instruction-following.
- Best for brainstorming variants and converting notes into draftable material.
- 1M context.

**Cons**
- Prose is competent rather than distinctive. Recurring judgment across sources: reads like solid commercial fiction, not authored work.
- Documented sycophancy and conflict-aversion problems — the "unprompted redemption arc" failure, where antagonists are softened against author intent.
- Naming is currently chaotic (Sol / Terra / Luna variants), making it hard to know which model you're actually invoking.

**Composite:** The outlining and logic-audit model. Do not trust for final prose. Route drafting elsewhere.

## 4. Claude Sonnet 5 — 8.3

**Pros**

- Up to 1M context — cheap enough to run whole-manuscript continuity passes without thinking about cost.

**Cons**

- Better for drafting and diagnosis than for final voice.

**Composite:** The workhorse. Most of a writing pipeline's token volume should run here, with the expensive models reserved for the passes that need them.

**Pros**
- Most personality-forward default of the group. Best for comedic fiction and satire.

## The one credible signal

Independent analyses produced by competing labs also rank Claude models first for creative prose. A model ranking a competitor above itself is more credible evidence than a model ranking itself first. That specific convergence carries information; the rest of the ordering should be treated as soft.

The defensible narrow claim: **Anthropic models have held a consistent edge on voice consistency across long documents in human evaluation, and that property is what fiction specifically needs.** On plot logic, GPT wins. On sourcing and context volume, Gemini wins.

# Part 2 — Trends

**1. Creative writing has split off as its own model tier.** Fable 5 is the first frontier model positioned for prose rather than reasoning. Expect other labs to follow. The coding-first optimization of 2024–25 was flattening prose in ways general benchmarks didn't capture.

**2. Prose benchmarks are improving but remain soft.** EQ-Bench now penalizes vocabulary-flexing, tracks repetition, and scores "slop" (frequency of LLM-overused phrasing). Real methodological progress, but still LLM-judged. Surge's Hemingway-bench took the opposite approach — expert human writers spending hours per model, on the argument that automated graders reward criteria-hacking and arena-style Elo rewards clickbait. That critique is correct and is supported by LitBench (see Part 3).

**3. The binding constraint moved from generation to continuity.** With 1M–2M contexts you can hold a whole manuscript plus bible in-window. The high-value use is no longer "write me a scene" — it's catching overused words, inconsistent character details, pacing drops, and voice drift.

**4. Voice files are standard practice and matter more than model choice.** Consistent finding across sources: a short explicit style document injected into every session does more for output distinctiveness than upgrading models does. The mode-collapse research (Part 3) explains why and suggests the effect is larger than commonly assumed.

**5. Stage-splitting is the professional workflow.** Outlining rewards structural reasoning; prose rewards voice and rhythm; few models are best at both. Serious users route by stage rather than picking one model.

**6. Disclosure is settled; detection isn't.** KDP, Apple Books, and Kobo have published AI disclosure requirements. Detectors remain inconsistent but are used as risk signals by stores, clients, and reviewers. Note the finding in Part 3 that fine-tuned output largely evades detection while prompted output does not.

## 3.1 Why creative capability diverged from general capability

**Source:** *Verbalized Sampling: How to Mitigate Mode Collapse and Unlock LLM Diversity.* Zhang, Yu, Chong, Sicilia, Tomz, Manning & Shi. Northeastern / Stanford / West Virginia. arXiv:2510.01171.
**Verification:** ✅ Read directly. All figures below confirmed against the paper.

### The claim it displaces

Prior work attributed mode collapse to algorithmic causes — that a single reward model can't capture diverse human preferences (Chakraborty et al. 2024), or that KL-regularized optimization amplifies majority-style responses (Xiao et al. 2024). Both imply the fix is better machinery.

This paper argues the cause is upstream of the machinery: **even with a perfect reward model and a perfect optimization process, bias inherent in the preference data would still drive mode collapse.**

### The mechanism

**Step 1 — Cognitive grounding.** Annotators favor familiar text for well-established psychological reasons: mere-exposure effect (Zajonc 1968) and availability heuristic (Tversky & Kahneman 1973) — frequent or easily recalled content feels more likely and is liked more; processing fluency (Alter & Oppenheimer 2009; Reber et al. 2004) — easy-to-process content is automatically perceived as more truthful and higher quality; schema congruity theory (Mandler) — information matching existing mental models is accepted with less critical thought.

**Step 2 — Formalization.** Reward is modeled as:

```
r(x,y) = r_true(x,y) + α · log π_ref(y|x) + ε(x)
```

True task utility, plus a typicality term weighted by α, plus noise. The proxy for typicality is the base model's log-likelihood — a model trained to maximize likelihood over massive corpora has probability scores that inherently encode textual typicality.

**Step 3 — Measurement.** Using HelpSteer, which rates responses separately for correctness and overall helpfulness, they formed **6,874 response pairs answering the same prompt with identical correctness ratings**, then computed per-token log-likelihoods under Llama 3.1 405B Base and GLM 4.5 Base.

Result: **α̂ = 0.57 ± 0.07 and 0.65 ± 0.07, both p < 10⁻¹⁴.**

The design is what makes this convincing. Holding correctness constant isolates typicality from quality. Annotators pick the more base-model-likely response *independent of whether it is more correct*.

**Step 4 — Derivation.** Substituting into the standard RLHF objective yields:

```
π*(y|x) ∝ π_ref(y|x)^γ · exp(r_true(x,y)/β),    where γ = 1 + α/β > 1
```

An exponent greater than 1 on a probability distribution is temperature scaling toward the mode. **Any positive typicality weight strictly sharpens the distribution.**

### Why this specifically kills fiction

When many responses are tied on true task utility — the normal condition in creative writing — the `exp` term cancels and you are left with `π* ∝ π_ref^γ`. Typicality bias becomes the **sole tiebreaker**.

- On a math problem, `r_true` is steep. One answer is right. α is noise.
- On "write the next sentence," `r_true` is flat across thousands of good options. α does all the work.

This is the mechanism behind every observed symptom: metronomic sentence-length rhythm, verbosity and adjective loading, telling rather than showing, unprompted redemption arcs for antagonists, and affective flattening where high-arousal emotional states are suppressed toward neutral.

It also explains why the degradation went unnoticed: general benchmarks measure tasks where `r_true` is steep, so they show no problem.

## 3.2 Verbalized Sampling — the inference-time mitigation

**Verification:** ✅ Read directly.

### Different prompts collapse to different modes

A collapsed model does not collapse to one place. The paper categorizes three prompt types:

| Type | Example | Collapses to |
|---|---|---|
| Instance-level | "Tell me a joke about coffee" | The mode instance of the base model |
| List-level | "Tell me 5 jokes about coffee" | A uniform distribution of related items |
| **Distribution-level (VS)** | "Tell me 5 jokes **with their probabilities**" | An approximation of the pretraining distribution |

### Evidence the probabilities are load-bearing

Models were asked to generate US states. The RedPajama pretraining corpus was queried to build a reference distribution of how often each state actually appears. Averaged over 10 trials, the VS-elicited distribution matched that reference with **KL divergence of 0.12** for Claude-4-Sonnet. Direct prompting collapsed to repeatedly emitting California and Texas.

The verbalized probabilities are not decorative — they are the handle that pulls the pretraining distribution back into reach.

### The prompt

```
System: You are a helpful assistant. For each query, please generate a set of
five possible responses, each within a separate <response> tag. Responses
should each include a <text> and a numeric <probability>. Please sample at
random from the tails of the distribution, such that the probability of each
response is less than 0.10.

User: [your actual request]
```

### Results, with correct framing

| Figure | What it actually measures |
|---|---|
| **1.6–2.1× diversity** | Semantic diversity = 1 − mean pairwise cosine similarity of response embeddings (text-embedding-3-small). Measured, not judged. |
| **25.7%** | Human study on **diversity**, not quality. 30 annotators, 90 output pairs, 4-point Likert. Poem 1.90→2.39, Story 2.74→3.06, Joke 1.83→3.01 (Direct → VS-Standard). The headline figure corresponds to the poem row. |
| **66.8%** | Recovery of base-model diversity, from the Tulu-3 ablation. |

⚠️ Note the second row. The paper's own contributions list describes 25.7% as "human evaluation scores," but the underlying table measures how *varied* the outputs are, not how *good*. Humans confirm greater variety; they were not asked whether it was better.

### The strongest evidence: the Tulu-3 ablation

Running the Tulu-3 checkpoints — base → SFT → DPO → RLVR — lets you watch collapse arrive in sequence:

| Stage | Direct prompting | VS |
|---|---|---|
| Base model diversity | 45.4% | 45.4% |
| After SFT | 20.8% | ~30% |
| After DPO | 10.8% | ~30% |
| Retained vs. base | **23.8%** | **66.8%** |

After DPO, VS outperforms direct prompting by **182.6%**. This is an ablation across training checkpoints, not a vibes comparison.

### Quality trade-off

VS-Standard quality remains comparable to baselines; VS-CoT achieves the highest diversity while maintaining high quality, pushing the Pareto front.

⚠️ Caveat: quality was scored by Claude-3.7-Sonnet using Creative Writing v3 rubrics. The diversity numbers are measured; the quality numbers are LLM-judged.

### Configuration

| Knob | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Probability threshold** | Continuous dial. Diversity increases as threshold decreases. Tunable in-prompt without touching decoding params. |
| **k (candidates)** | Higher k → more diversity, but quality degrades if k too large. Paper used k=5. |
| **Variants** | VS-Standard, VS-CoT (reason first), VS-Multi (across turns). CoT and Multi both beat Standard on diversity. |
| **Decoding** | Orthogonal to temperature, top-p, min-p. Combines with them; not a substitute. |
| **Format** | "probability" best for VS-Standard/CoT; "confidence" best for VS-Multi. |
| **Cognitive burden** | VS-Standard costs some quality, less severe on larger models. VS-CoT and VS-Multi overcome it entirely and *improve* quality on large models. |
| **Scaling** | Larger models achieve diversity gains **1.5–2× greater** than smaller models. |

### Qualitative illustration

Prompt: "Without a goodbye." Direct prompting produced three stories about a romantic partner vanishing — all three named her Elara, all three featuring a cold side of the bed. VS produced an email from a childhood friend relocating to Patagonia, the last star in the universe going out, and a DJ collapsing mid-set at a blues club.

That is the difference between rerolling and resampling. Rerolling returns Elara with a different mug.

### Limitations

- Tested on poem continuation, story generation, and joke writing — all short-form, all cold-start. **Not tested on scene-level drafting inside an established manuscript with a fixed voice and continuity bible.**
- Costs k× output tokens per call.
- "Diversity" is semantic distance, not quality. The tails contain the fresh option *and* the incoherent one.
- Benefit is model-scale dependent.

## 3.3 Evaluation benchmarks

### LitBench

**Source:** Fein, Russo, Xiang, Jolly, Rafailov & Haber (Stanford). arXiv:2507.00769, 1 July 2025.
**Verification:** ✅ Confirmed against abstract and paper listing.

First standardized benchmark and paired dataset for creative writing verification. Held-out test set of **2,480 debiased human-labeled story comparisons** drawn from Reddit's r/WritingPrompts, plus a **43,827-pair training corpus** of human preference labels. Debiased against length, temporal, and popularity effects.

Key findings:

- Strongest off-the-shelf zero-shot judge: **Claude-3.7-Sonnet at 73% agreement** with human preferences.
- Trained Bradley-Terry and generative reward models: **78% accuracy**, outperforming all zero-shot judges.
- Small and open-source LLM judges fail to evaluate creative writing accurately; some leading proprietary models are competitive with trained verifiers.

**Implication:** the ceiling on off-the-shelf LLM-judged creative evaluation is roughly 73% human agreement. Any leaderboard built on zero-shot LLM judging inherits that error rate. This is the strongest available argument against confident fine-grained rankings of creative writing models — including the ones in Part 1 of this document.

### WritingBench

**Verification:** ⚠️ Not independently verified this session. Reported specifications below come from secondary sources.

Reported as 1,239 queries spanning 6 primary domains and 100 subdomains, using query-dependent evaluation where instance-specific assessment criteria are generated per prompt and scored by a fine-tuned critic model, with a reported 87% human agreement rate. The query-dependent design is a genuine methodological advance over static rubrics — a rubric built for technical documents cannot evaluate a speculative fiction scene. Treat the specific figures as unconfirmed.

### EQ-Bench Creative Writing

**Verification:** ⚠️ Leaderboard values could not be retrieved directly (JS-rendered). Reported positions vary wildly across aggregators — see Part 0.

Methodologically the most improved of the automated creative benchmarks: penalizes vocabulary-flexing, tracks repetition, applies degradation penalties, and scores "slop" (frequency of LLM-characteristic phrasing). Uses a Claude model as judge, which is a structural conflict when ranking Claude-family models.

## 3.4 Style transfer: prompting vs. fine-tuning

**Sources:**
1. Chakrabarty & Dhillon, *Can Good Writing Be Generative? Expert-Level AI Writing Emerges through Fine-Tuning on High-Quality Books.* CHI '26, Barcelona, 13–17 April 2026. arXiv:2601.18353 / DOI 10.1145/3772318.3791276
2. Chakrabarty, Ginsburg & Dhillon, *Readers Prefer Outputs of AI Trained on Copyrighted Books over Expert Human Writers.* arXiv:2510.13939 (Stony Brook / Columbia Law / Michigan)

**Verification:** ✅ Confirmed. Note these are two related papers from overlapping teams — the CHI paper is the behavioral study; the second is the copyright-focused analysis with a larger lay-judge pool.

### Design

28 MFA-trained writers competed against three frontier LLMs (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) in emulating 50 critically acclaimed authors — Nobel laureates, Booker winners, National Book Award finalists. Excerpts up to 450 words. Blind pairwise comparisons by 28 expert judges and 131 lay judges (516 general readers in the preregistered companion study).

### The inversion

| Condition | Expert preference |
|---|---|
| **In-context prompting** | Experts preferred human writing in **82.7%** of comparisons |
| **Fine-tuned** | Experts preferred **AI** in **62%** of comparisons |

Lay judges preferred AI writing across both conditions.

In the preregistered companion study, in-context prompted AI was strongly disfavored by MFA-trained readers on both stylistic fidelity (OR = 0.16) and writing quality (OR = 0.13).

### Details that matter and are often omitted

- **The fine-tuned model was GPT-4o**, chosen because it was the only frontier model among the three permitting fine-tuning via API. This is not a frontier-model result.
- **Fine-tuning on as few as two books was sufficient.**
- **Detection asymmetry:** fine-tuned outputs were flagged as AI-generated roughly **3%** of the time, versus **97%** for in-context prompted output.
- Debrief interviews reported that the fidelity of fine-tuned mimicry triggered an identity crisis among expert writers and eroded aesthetic confidence.

### The framing correction

This is frequently summarized as a productivity finding — "fine-tuning beats prompting, so fine-tune." That inverts the authors' conclusion. The paper argues that fine-tuned LLMs built on an individual author's collected copyrighted works **should not qualify as fair use** when used to produce style-emulating output, and that market substitution and dilution become plausible rather than abstract under the fourth fair use factor.

**The finding is that it works well enough to be a legal problem.**

### The legally clean version

Fine-tuning on **your own** corpus carries none of this exposure. The "two books is sufficient" result is the encouraging number for a working author with a modest backlist. A log of accepted-versus-rejected drafting choices is a cleaner training signal than raw manuscript text, and can be accumulated as a byproduct of normal work.

# Part 4 — Practical application

## 4.1 Route by stage, not by preference

| Stage | Model class | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Research / sourcing | Gemini 3.5 Pro | Context volume, citation tooling |
| Outline / structure | GPT-5.6 or DeepSeek V4 | Plot logic; DeepSeek at a fraction of the cost |
| Prose drafting | Claude Fable 5 / Opus 5 | Voice, subtext, interiority |
| Continuity / consistency | Claude Sonnet 5 or DeepSeek V4 | Cheap enough to run at manuscript scale |
| Line edit | Sonnet 5 | Fast, cheap, adequate |

Don't pay frontier prose rates for mechanical passes. Don't run outlining models on final prose.

**Genre note.** Romance is where the expensive prose model earns its cost — the genre lives on interiority, which is exactly what affective flattening destroys. Fantasy is the opposite: the expensive problem is continuity across a world bible, which cheap large-context models solve.

## 4.2 Gate Verbalized Sampling by task type

Straight from the math: VS pays off only where `r_true` is flat. Where a right answer exists, VS costs 5× tokens for nothing and can degrade output.

**VS on:** scene drafting, opening lines, character decisions and motivations, dialogue variants, premise expansion, exemplar generation.

**VS off:** continuity checks, outline validation, line edits, copyedit passes, fact verification — anything with a verifiable output.

## 4.3 Two-stage VS for long-form

The paper tested short outputs. Requesting five 1,200-word scenes in one call will hit token limits and degrade later candidates — the same failure mode as k-too-large, triggered by length instead of count.

- **Stage 1:** VS with k=5 over scene *approaches*, 2–3 sentences each. Cheap, short, and this is where mode collapse actually bites — the choice of *what happens* is where typicality does its damage.
- **Stage 2:** draft the selected approach at full length with ordinary direct prompting.

Distribution benefit at the decision point, without paying 5× on prose generation.

## 4.4 Implementation notes

- **Compose, don't replace.** Craft/style instructions govern content; VS governs sampling and output envelope. Put VS instructions last, since they define the response format.
- **Tolerant parsing.** Models emit near-XML. Wrap a strict parser with a regex fallback. Validate count, presence of probabilities, threshold compliance.
- **Fail open.** Malformed output after one retry falls back to direct prompting, flagged as degraded. VS is an enhancement, not a dependency.
- **Discard probabilities after validation.** They're the mechanism, not downstream information.
- **Use VS-CoT on production-grade models**, VS-Standard only if cost-constrained.
- **Validate on the production model.** Because gains scale with model capability, VS measured on a cheap test model will *understate* production performance — potentially by enough to talk you out of shipping it.
- **Instrument it.** Semantic diversity is cheap to compute yourself: mean pairwise cosine similarity of candidate embeddings, reported as 1 − s̄. Log it per generation, plus tokens-per-accepted-output for VS vs. direct. Within weeks you'll have your own numbers on the workload nobody has published on.
- **Surface candidates, never auto-select.** The selection log becomes a preference dataset of your own taste — and, per §3.4, eventually a legally clean fine-tuning corpus. Design the schema early.

# Part 5 — What remains untested

1. **VS on long-form drafting within an established manuscript.** All published results are short-form cold-start. This is the single biggest open question for working novelists.
2. **VS interaction with anti-fingerprint / style-constraint prompting.** Constraints narrow the distribution; VS widens it. Whether these compose or partially cancel is unknown. Requires a four-cell A/B: baseline / constraints only / VS only / both.
3. **Whether the July 2026 model rankings survive independent evaluation.** Opus 5 and Fable 5 are both too new for a review base.
4. **Whether fine-tuning on a small personal corpus reproduces the MFA-study effect.** The published result used complete works of acclaimed authors, with a minimum of two books. A single-author backlist plus a selection log is a different and untested data profile.
